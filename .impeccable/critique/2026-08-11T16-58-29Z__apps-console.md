## Design-specificity verdict

The console feels authored for PDPP, not category-interchangeable. Ink Carbon defines protocol blue, owner-action copper, paper geometry, and explicit system states. The shell's Collection, Sharing, and Server grouping is owner-specific. The system is not fully converged: key overview and setup surfaces use medium/extra-large rounded cards despite the design system's square sheets and 2px control radius.

The deterministic detector returned 16 raw warnings for one rule, `side-tab`, around `border-l-4` status callouts. Its report duplicated locations; direct source verification found seven unique sites. Every site is an error/success/status boundary, so the “AI slop” characterization is a false positive. No high-severity detector rule fired.

## Overall impression

PDPP's strongest product quality is truthfulness: source health, recovery, and Explorer avoid pretending unknowns are healthy or complete. The largest opportunity is decision design. The console often exposes the full operator model before a first-time owner has earned it, while the highest-trust decision—grant approval—shows too little.

## Cognitive load

Five of eight checklist items fail: single focus, chunking, one thing at a time, minimal choices, and working-memory continuity. Grouping, visual hierarchy, and progressive disclosure pass.

Confirmed decision points above four visible options:

- 12 primary navigation destinations;
- five grant-status filters;
- four date presets plus custom range in Explorer;
- up to eight Explorer typeahead suggestions;
- an unbounded source catalog and dynamic connection/stream facets.

## What's working

- Source health and recovery separate status, next action, navigation, and destruction. Records/history retention is stated instead of implied.
- Explorer keeps filters URL-backed, supports real keyboard behavior, and avoids false completeness claims.
- Accessibility foundations exist: global focus rings, textual status equivalents, labeled forms, live regions, semantic buttons, and guarded destructive actions.
- Responsive source code uses breakpoints, overflow containment, truncation, and wrapping for long identifiers and tables.

## Priority issues

### P1 — grant approval precedes adequate review

`apps/console/src/app/(console)/grants/page.tsx:177` exposes Approve and Deny from a row showing an ID, time, client caption, source, and stream names. It does not present the request purpose, duration, field scope, or a final confirmation.

Why it matters: an owner can make the product's highest-trust decision without enough information to understand what is being granted.

Fix: make Review request the primary action. Present human-readable purpose, duration/retention, sources, streams, and fields, then require an explicit final approval.

Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`.

### P1 — mobile navigation is not a contained modal interaction

`packages/pdpp-brand-react/src/shell-frame.tsx:294` renders the mobile drawer with Escape and backdrop-close behavior but without dialog semantics, focus-on-open, focus containment/restoration, or an inert background.

Why it matters: a keyboard or screen-reader user can tab behind the visible drawer and operate obscured controls.

Fix: use the existing dialog primitive or implement `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal`, initial focus, tab containment, focus restoration, and inert background behavior.

Suggested command: `$impeccable audit`.

### P2 — first setup is a catalog, not an onboarding decision

`apps/console/src/app/(console)/components/source-setup-catalog.tsx:282` presents all available connector cards, existing accounts, next actions, optional acquisition paths, documentation, and a separate experimental group.

Why it matters: a first-time owner must choose a provider before the product helps them identify the shortest credible first win.

Fix: lead with “Start with one source” and one to three recommended paths based on availability and reliability. Put the complete catalog behind Browse all sources.

Suggested command: `$impeccable onboard`.

### P2 — Explorer over-compresses the first decision

`apps/console/src/app/(console)/explore/explore-canvas.tsx:1392` presents query language, sort, date, options, saved views, facets, include/exclude controls, results, and inspection in one workbench.

Why it matters: the surface is excellent for an expert but forces a novice or returning owner to reconstruct the query model before browsing a record.

Fix: establish a simple browse default with source and date visible; place typed operators, negation, saved-view management, and advanced sort behind an explicit advanced mode.

Suggested command: `$impeccable distill`.

### P2 — implementation drifts from the product's semantic token and geometry system

Rounded panels conflict with Ink Carbon's square-paper rules. Hard-coded amber, emerald, and blue Tailwind values also recur outside the semantic token system, including connection status, enrollment, and schedule surfaces.

Why it matters: visual state meanings and dark-mode behavior can drift, and the console's distinctive language becomes less credible between routes.

Fix: decide and document the permitted rounding exceptions; migrate status colors to semantic tokens and verify both themes.

Suggested commands: `$impeccable colorize`, then `$impeccable polish`.

### P3 — loading motion lacks an explicit reduced-motion branch

`apps/console/src/app/(console)/components/route-loading.tsx:27` uses `animate-pulse`, and the stream viewer defines a spinner animation without a source-visible reduced-motion alternative.

Why it matters: status motion should remain legible without forcing continuous animation on motion-sensitive users.

Fix: preserve the status change with a non-animated visual alternative under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

Suggested command: `$impeccable animate`.

## Persona red flags

- **Alex, power user:** Explorer is strong, but source fleet work remains one selected connection at a time; no bulk health review/action path is visible.
- **Sam, keyboard/screen-reader user:** focus rings and textual status labels are good, but mobile navigation loses modal containment at the breakpoint where the desktop sidebar disappears.
- **Jordan, first-time owner:** the overview has the right Add a data source CTA, then immediately asks for a provider choice amid experimental/browser/acquisition terminology without a recommendation.

## Minor observations

- Many source tests are regex/source invariants. They preserve intent but cannot prove focus order, responsive layout, contrast, or completed tasks.
- Explorer's exclusion symbol has an accessible label/title, but visual discoverability still depends heavily on hover and symbol interpretation.
- Diagnostics are truthful but appear after Streams and Recent runs; the header repair action partly mitigates that distance.

## Questions to consider

1. What must an owner understand and explicitly affirm before Approve can be safe?
2. Could the first success be “connect one recommended source and see its first record” instead of “choose from the whole catalog”?
3. Should Explorer be one workbench for everyone, or reveal query-language power after a successful simple browse?
